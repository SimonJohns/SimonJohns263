Water Usage

  • Data centers rely heavily on water, for cooling their systems and indirectly through the electricity they consume. A normal medium sized data center may use around 110 million gallons of water a year and some data centers can even use up to 5 million gallons a day and 1.8 billion a year. This amount could support thousands of people or farms. Beyond the water used on site to cool the data center, a large share of total consumption comes from the energy required to run these facilities. Since about 56% of U.S. data center electricity is generated from fossil fuels, much of their water use is tied to power plants that rely on steam and cooling systems. Some estimates suggest that this indirect usage reached 211 billion gallons in 2023 (Environmental and Energy Study Institute).

  • This level of consumption is concerning given how limited usable freshwater is. Although water covers most of the planet, only about 3% is freshwater, and 0.5% is easily accessible for human use (Environmental and Energy Study Institute). As demand for cloud computing and AI continues to grow, data centers require more energy, which in turn drives higher water usage for cooling those data centers.
