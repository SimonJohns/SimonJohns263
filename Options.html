Options for Action

  • Option 1: Don’t use Data Centers at all

    Option 2: Use them as is, and keep expanding to build more

    Option 3: Use them, but in a limited capacity, and try and develop ways to limit environmental impact

    • In the rights lens, option 3 respects the rights of all the stakeholders the most. Option 1 does not respect the rights of the companies and the people who use the services provided, which is a large majority of the stakeholders and is thus not supported by the rights lens. Option 2 does not respect the rights of the stakeholders whoa re directly impacted by the environmental effects of data centers, and therefore in the rights lens this is not supported. Option 3 however allows each stakeholder group to continue, you can’t just delete the problem by getting rid of the centers, but you can at least try and limit the impact, allowing each stakeholder to benefit, or at least not be completely ignored.

    The Utilitarianism lens is debatable, for option the environmental impact of data centers is negated, however the benefits that come from these data centers are also negated. So many people benefit from them in their daily internet use and arguably it does harm for people to not be able to use them, however the long term impacts of not having the negative impact of data centers, suggest that it may support option 1. For option 2, the negative impact of data centers and not even trying to limit that outweighs the benefits and the long term possibility of harm towards everybody on the planet outweighs the benefits, so it does not support option 2. Option 3, it supports both giving access to the benefits from data centers, while it also works toward limiting them and making sure that it data centers are implemented in ways to protect the environment. Option 3 has the strongestcase of support for the Utilitarianism lens.

    In the short term the Communitarinism lens does not support option 1, as it completely ignores the majority of people who want to use the services provided, while preferring the people living around the datacenters. However in the long term, hypothetically, if the environmental impacts are never curbed, then it could impact everybody. However I think it still doesn’t support that as it is far in the future and not confirmed. For option 2, it supports the idea of giving maximum access and support to datacenters because it benefits the vast majority of the stakeholders, while sacrificing a few. Option 3 is not as supported, because limiting the services, doesnt benefit the majority, even if its not fully and does benefit the people living around the data centers.
